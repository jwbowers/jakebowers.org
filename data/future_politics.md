PS 374 (Future Politics) is a seminar that pairs social science and political philosophy with science fiction. Students read these together, discuss them, and build concepts for how politics changes as technology, environments, and populations change --- and practice imagining alternative political, social, and economic futures. The most recent syllabus is here: [PS 374 Fall 2025 syllabus](static/syllabi/future-politics-ps374-fall-2025.pdf).

William Gibson once said, "We live in an incomprehensible present. And that what I'm actually trying to do is illuminate the moment. And make the moment accessible. I am not even really trying to explain the moment. Just trying to make it accessible." Teachers help students ready themselves for a future and thrive in a present, so I worry about how to live in and shape both. Politics will and should change as technology, environments, and populations change. Undergraduates at the University of Illinois and I read canonical political theorists alongside science fiction authors so we can judge the politics of an incomprehensible present and an unpredictable future. We practice imagining a future politics to build a flexible political imagination --- following [Jamais Cascio's idea](http://www.openthefuture.com/) that [futurism is not prediction but mental readiness](http://www.fastcompany.com/1362037/futures-thinking-basics). [We had some minutes of fame.](http://chrisblattman.com/2014/09/02/best-political-science-syllabus-ever/) You can see our syllabi from [2021](https://static.jakebowers.org/MISC/ps374f21.pdf), [2020](https://static.jakebowers.org/MISC/ps374f20.pdf), and [2013](https://static.jakebowers.org/MISC/ps300s13syl.pdf). You can see an [overview of the Future Politics class here](https://las.illinois.edu/news/2020-06-01/fiction-politics-and-possible-future).

### Talks, Interviews, and Collaborations

- Joined other political scientists at the [Politics of the Future Workshop](https://web.archive.org/web/20250910105425/https://sites.duke.edu/politicsofthefutureworkshop/) at Duke in June 2014 to ask what new politics demands of political science.
- Spoke at the "Brave New World" conference at the [Casa da Musica](http://www.casadamusica.com/) in Porto, Portugal, in June 2015, funded by the Francisco Manuel dos Santos Foundation. Bruce Sterling and I closed the conference [in conversation](https://youtu.be/PtliqBuI-GA).
- Talked with the Portuguese newspaper [Publico](http://www.publico.pt/politica/noticia/a-critica-da-politica-do-presente-esta-na-ficcao-cientifica-1698270) about reading science fiction alongside political theory as preparation for the future.
- Drafted, with [Cara Wong](http://carawong.org), an [essay](https://medium.com/@jakebowers/thoughts-on-the-internet-of-kids-things-2463a6884460) on what belongs in a child's room in an Internet of Things (IoT) home, after visiting [Casa Jasmina](https://web.archive.org/web/20170130190619/http://casajasmina.arduino.cc/) in Turin.
- Argued on Australian National Radio's [Science Fiction: Earth's repair manual?](http://www.abc.net.au/radionational/programs/futuretense/science-fiction3a-earth27s-repair-manual3f-ep/5899310) that science fiction sharpens political thinking about the future, with detours on why Marx is worth reading alongside science fiction.
- Helped [John Ahlquist](http://johnahlquist.net/) conceive the [UCSD San Diego 2049 event](https://web.archive.org/web/20210929070213/http://imagination.ucsd.edu/_wp/programs/san-diego-2049/).
